Worm bin math

A worm bin goes wrong in three places: the scrap supply guessed from how the kitchen feels instead of how it actually cooks, the bin bought by its catalog photo instead of by the feed rate, and the harvest daydreamed in buckets instead of measured in liters. Worm bin math turns the kitchen into the scraps, turns the scraps into the bin, and turns the bin into the castings. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Bin plan - the people in the kitchen at a labeled scrap rate and a labeled feed rate become the square meters of bin.
  • Worm plan - the bin area at a labeled stocking rate becomes the grams of worms to start with.
  • Harvest plan - the bin area at a labeled monthly yield becomes the liters of castings to expect.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 0.5 kg of scraps per person a week, a 3.5 kg per m2 weekly feed rate, 500 g of worms per m2 and 5 L of castings per m2 a month - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Weigh a real week of scraps before you buy anything, keep meat, dairy and oils out of the bin, and a sealed bin with drainage keeps pests out. This is planning arithmetic, not a biology lab.
